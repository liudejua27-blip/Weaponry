# Research to Design Prior

Use this boundary when Codex researches knife art, FPS presentation, Three.js techniques, or licensed examples.
The purpose is to expand the design vocabulary without pretending that web material reveals the hidden truth of a
particular reference.

## Admission pipeline

1. Record the page/repository URL, retrieval date, author/publisher, license and the exact claim being considered.
2. Classify the claim:
   - `observed` is reserved for user-authorized reference evidence;
   - `design-prior` is a reusable visual grammar, normalized ratio or composition cue;
   - `inferred` is one plausible completion of missing structure;
   - `original-choice` is a deliberate new design decision;
   - `unknown` remains unresolved.
3. Abstract the claim into normalized visual units. Do not import manufacturing dimensions, weapon performance,
   machining instructions or real-world operating advice.
4. Admit it only through a successor `KnifeKnowledge@1` or an explicitly classified program field. Preserve the
   source record and uncertainty; never rewrite it as reference observation.
5. Code, textures, meshes, fonts and shaders require separate license/provenance review. A public URL is not a
   license. Prefer implementing an idea through Weaponry's closed grammar rather than copying an asset.
6. A new prior may generate candidates, but it cannot approve them. The active ObjectiveFunction and readiness
   evaluator still decide structural eligibility from reproducible evidence.

## Useful research outputs

- silhouette family and curve rhythm;
- normalized attachment ratios;
- semantic Part decomposition;
- MaterialZone contrast/readability cues;
- FPS viewport occupancy priors;
- bounded Three.js primitive, loft, sweep, curve and shader techniques;
- common failure modes and correction scopes.

## Rejected outputs

- a claim that one image uniquely determines hidden geometry;
- copied commercial art without usable rights;
- arbitrary executable scripts, URLs or network fetches inside a canonical program;
- a quality, likeness or commercial PASS derived from prose alone;
- real-world weapon construction or performance information.
